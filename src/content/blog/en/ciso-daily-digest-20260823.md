## TikTok Agrees to $400M U.S. Child-Privacy Settlement

On **August 22, 2026**, TikTok reached a **$400 million** court-approved settlement resolving a U.S. class-action lawsuit alleging the platform collected and processed data from users under 13 without parental consent, in violation of the **Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA)**. The suit centered on TikTok's handling of minors' personal information — including persistent identifiers used for targeted advertising — and the adequacy of its age-gating controls.

The settlement is one of the largest child-privacy resolutions in the consumer-app sector and follows a pattern of escalating regulatory pressure on platforms that monetize underage user data.

### Why This Reshapes Privacy Governance

For CISOs, the case reinforces three operational realities: (1) **data minimization** for minors is now a litigated, not theoretical, obligation — collect-by-default architectures carry direct financial liability; (2) **age-assurance and parental-consent controls** must be auditable, not cosmetic; and (3) the **blurred line between advertising identifiers and personal data** means telemetry pipelines feeding ad systems are in-scope for privacy compliance. Organizations building consumer or education-facing products should treat COPPA-adjacent data flows as a board-level risk, not a legal-department footnote.

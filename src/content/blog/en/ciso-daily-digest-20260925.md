## North Korean Attackers Steal $351.6 Million from Bitget Crypto Exchange After Compromising Backend Infrastructure

Cryptocurrency exchange **Bitget disclosed that suspected North Korean threat actors compromised its backend wallet infrastructure** and stole **$351.6 million in cryptocurrency assets** across multiple blockchains. The attack, detected on September 24, 2026, at 18:31 UTC, involved unauthorized transfers from Bitget's hot and warm wallets containing ETH, XRP, BNB, AVAX, USDT, and USDC across Ethereum, XRP Ledger, Arbitrum, Avalanche, Optimism, BSC, and Base networks. **Bitget's cold wallets and the majority of platform assets remain secure**, and customer account balances are accurate; however, withdrawals were temporarily suspended while a comprehensive security review by **Mandiant and SlowMist** is underway. According to **Bitget CEO Gracy Chen**, the attacker compromised a **critical backend system within the wallet infrastructure, spoofed transaction data, and triggered authorization processes** to move funds. **No further unauthorized transfers are possible**, and the attack was contained within the hot wallet segment. Based on **IP behavior patterns and on-chain analysis**, Bitget attributes the attack to known North Korean hacker organizations; **TRM Labs and Elliptic have identified overlaps with wallets used to launder previous DPRK-attributed hacks**, including the 2025 Bybit breach ($1.5 billion) and KelpDAO LayerZero bridge theft ($292 million). If confirmed, **2026 would reach $1.04 billion in North Korean crypto theft** — the second-largest year on record after 2025's $1.68 billion, signaling an escalation in scale and frequency of attacks against the cryptocurrency ecosystem.

### Why This Reshapes Cryptocurrency Risk Management for Financial Institutions

- **Backend infrastructure is now the primary attack surface.** Unlike account takeovers or key theft, the Bitget attack compromised internal wallet authorization systems — showing that attackers are shifting from customer-facing vectors to internal operational infrastructure where a single compromised system can unlock millions in value.
- **Cold storage assurances matter only if hot wallets are sized appropriately.** Bitget's statement that cold wallets are "unaffected" is correct but cold comfort; the exchange's hot wallet (designed for operational liquidity) was drained completely. Cryptocurrency exchanges must drastically reduce hot-wallet balances and move to hardware-backed settlement for large transfers.
- **On-chain forensics expose launderer patterns.** TRM Labs and Elliptic's identification of wallet overlaps with previous DPRK attacks demonstrates that attackers reuse laundering infrastructure across incidents. Financial institutions and blockchain platforms can now track these overlaps and freeze addresses in real time.
- **2026's stolen volume is approaching 2025's annual total in just nine months.** The acceleration suggests either improved attack capabilities, reduced defensive maturity across targets, or a shift in adversary priorities toward higher-value targets. DPRK's cryptocurrency theft program is now a major source of revenue for the regime.

## Active Threats This Week

📌 **Roundcube Webmail CVE-2026-48842: Pre-Auth SQL Injection Actively Exploited in the Wild**
The **Canadian Centre for Cyber Security has warned that CVE-2026-48842**, a pre-authentication SQL injection in Roundcube Webmail versions 1.6.x before 1.6.16 and 1.7.x before 1.7.1, is being actively exploited. The vulnerability stems from a **preg_replace() backslash escape bypass** in the virtuser_query plugin that allows unauthenticated attackers to inject arbitrary SQL statements into Roundcube's database backend. Successful exploitation can expose **mail account credentials and stored messages**. Roundcube patches were released in May 2026 (versions 1.6.16 and 1.7.1), but **Shadowserver Foundation data shows over 523,000 Roundcube instances exposed to the internet**, with at least 10 flagged as vulnerable as of September 23, 2026. This high exposure combined with active exploitation makes Roundcube an attractive target for threat actors seeking to harvest sensitive email communications.
🔗 **Reference:** [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/roundcube-pre-auth-sql-injection-flaw.html)

📌 **Ukrainian Business Websites Hijacked to Serve Fake Cloudflare ClickFix Lures for Psychedelic Stealer**
An active ClickFix campaign has **compromised legitimate Ukrainian business websites** to inject **bogus Cloudflare verification pages** that trick visitors into downloading a previously undocumented information stealer called **Psychedelic**. The attack chain uses an **msiexec.exe command to fetch a Windows MSI installer** that delivers the stealer malware. Psychedelic is designed to **harvest browser passwords, account tokens, and cryptocurrency-wallet data**, set up scheduled-task persistence, and contact a command-and-control server for additional tasking. **Arctic Wolf Labs identified 557 views, 426 clicks, and 79 complete events across 32 countries**, with Ukraine accounting for 446 views, 351 clicks, and 71 complete events. Compromised Ukrainian sites include a hair-treatment clinic, scale-model manufacturer, specialist bookseller, psychological facility, tool retailer, and automotive retailer. **Russian-language branding and implementation artifacts suggest Russian operators** with heavy targeting focus on Ukrainian users.
🔗 **Reference:** [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/hacked-ukrainian-sites-serve-fake.html)

📌 **Unpatched OnePlus Flaws Allow Any Installed App to Gain Root Access Without Special Permissions**
Researcher **Rasmus Moorats has demonstrated that two unpatched flaws in OnePlus 15 running the latest OxygenOS can be chained to grant root access** to any malicious app the user installs — with **no special permissions requested and no prompts shown to the user**. The attack chain exploits two OnePlus services: **AtlasService**, which runs as root and accepts unauthenticated calls from any app, and **olc2**, a hardware helper that executes shell instructions if the caller is already root. The first flaw provides root access within a restricted dumpstate zone; the second flaw then escalates to full system-level Linux privileges, including kernel code loading. **OnePlus confirmed both flaws in May 2026** and set out an exclusive right to control disclosure, warning Moorats of legal liability if he published without permission. When OnePlus failed to release a fix by September, Moorats published anyway on September 24, 2026. **No CVE has been assigned, and no OnePlus advisory exists** — leaving all affected OxygenOS 16 devices (including OnePlus 12 Pro and older models) vulnerable to any installed malware without patches. OnePlus also stated the same flaws affect OPPO devices, though it did not specify which models.
🔗 **Reference:** [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/unpatched-oneplus-flaws-let-installed.html)

📌 **Placeholder Domain third-party[.]com Weaponized for ClickFix Supply-Chain Attacks Across 1,700+ Repositories**
The **non-reserved placeholder domain "third-party[.]com"** — commonly used in documentation, test code, and skill configurations as a generic example endpoint — **has been registered by attackers and now serves a ClickFix lure** to Windows browsers while displaying a harmless decoy to macOS and other users. A search on GitHub reveals the domain is **referenced in over 1,700 public repositories**, including AI agent skills and MCP-server documentation that cite it as an example endpoint. Unlike IANA-reserved placeholders such as "example[.]com," third-party[.]com was never protected, making it squattable and vulnerable to abuse. Windows users visiting the page see a **fake Cloudflare verification that poisons the clipboard and instructs them to paste a PowerShell command into the Run dialog**, fetching and executing a remote payload. **Manifold Security also identified 13 additional unprotected placeholder domains** serving malicious content, with "your-domain[.]com" and "yoursite[.]com" showing fake MacOS Security Center scareware and counterfeit investment schemes. The scale of exposure — hundreds of thousands of GitHub files and hundreds of agent skills referencing these domains — means attackers gain access to supply-chain code execution paths without any static security checks detecting the compromise.
🔗 **Reference:** [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/placeholder-third-partycom-referenced.html)

📌 **ClickFix Delivers RemotePanel and BoundSiphon: Modular .NET Malware for Persistent Access and Credential Theft**
**Blackpoint Cyber identified two previously undocumented .NET malware components delivered together via ClickFix**: **RemotePanel**, a persistent remote-access platform, and **BoundSiphon**, a credential and cryptocurrency stealer. **RemotePanel establishes persistence by masquerading as the Windows Time service** and gives operators broad control including PowerShell, file and process management, screen access, modular hidden virtual network computing (hVNC), and fleet management. **BoundSiphon runs primarily from memory** and targets browser credentials, sessions, cryptocurrency wallets, password manager data, and selected documents — including secrets protected by **Chromium App-Bound Encryption**. RemotePanel uses a **BNB Smart Chain contract to resolve its C2 server**, allowing threat actors to rotate infrastructure without rebuilding the malware. The attack sequence begins with a ClickFix command that uses PowerShell to initiate a multi-stage chain, abusing **CMSTPLUA COM object to bypass User Account Control (UAC)** and gain elevated administrative privileges without prompting. The process configures broad Microsoft Defender exclusions and fetches both payloads using different methods. Blackpoint recovered artifacts suggesting a **possible Russian-speaking development environment**.
🔗 **Reference:** [Dark Reading](https://www.darkreading.com/application-security/salesbleed-exploits-salesforce-agents-slack-phishing)

The summer period also saw a rise in ransomware targeting critical infrastructure, healthcare, and government sectors, with payment demands reaching $10+ million per incident.
